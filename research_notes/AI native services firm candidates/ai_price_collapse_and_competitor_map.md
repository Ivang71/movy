## Key Question 1: Documented price-collapse cases (old human-service price vs. new software price, with dates)

### Takeaway
Across at least ten categories the "software price" is now 1–10% of the former human-service price, and the collapse is concentrated in work where the AI performs the entire task with no one bearing liability for the output. The clearest documented US cases are real-estate transaction coordination ($250–500/file human vs. $14.99/contract AI), transcription ($1.99 vs. $0.25 per audio minute at Rev), translation ($0.15–0.25/word human vs. $0.001–0.01 raw AI, $0.04–0.12 MTPE), bookkeeping ($300–5,000/mo human vs. $99/mo AI-only at Pilot), AI SDR ($36K/yr vs. $120–200K loaded human), customer-support tickets ($5–15 human vs. $0.49–0.99 AI resolution), legal document drafting ($500–1,500 attorney NDA vs. $19.99/mo AI), tax prep ($220 simple 1040 vs. free–$200 software), resume writing ($150–600 vs. free–$50), and stock photography (Shutterstock content revenue −5% YoY, Getty bonds at 47c).

### Inferences
- The price ratio is the signal: where a liability-free AI product exists, price falls 90–99% (TC $14.99 vs $250–500; Rev 8x; translation 15–100x; legal $19.99/mo vs $500+). Where the AI-native provider carries liability (Crosby with malpractice cover at $250–1,000/contract), the discount vs. attorneys is only ~50–80%, not 98%.
- Volume and price move in opposite directions in the same category (Upwork data-entry/transcription volume +19% MoM while per-minute pay sits at $1.20; translation tech +12–18% while traditional −2%): AI expands the quantity of low-value work while destroying its unit price. Entrants compete on volume economics, not price.
- Within a single vendor (Pilot), the AI-only bookkeeping tier is $99/mo while the human-signed tax tier is $1,000–2,450+/yr — a clean within-firm demonstration that the signed, liability-bearing deliverable held price while the unsigned one collapsed.

## Key Question 2: Services where price is holding despite AI, and the structural reason

### Takeaway
Fees priced on outcomes (contingency), on a licensed signature, or on an insured liability have not collapsed: collections still run 10–50% of recovery, property-tax appeals 25–50% of first-year savings, R&D credits 25–40%, medical billing 4–10% of collections, SOC 2 attestations $15.5K–$200K, certified translation $0.15–0.50/word, and the AI-native recruiter Mercor charges a conventional 30% placement fee. The caveat is that AI is compressing the low end of several of these (63% of medical billers now charge ≤7.99%; AI collection vendors advertise 5–15%; a $49 flat-fee AI property-tax filer exists), so "holding" means the pricing model survives, not that every provider does.

### Cited Findings

**Medical billing (percentage of collections)**
- 2026 rates: "4% to 10% of net collections"; most small/mid practices 5–8%; cardiology/oncology/orthopedics 8–12%; per-claim $3–12; flat $500–2,500/mo; hybrid base + 3–5% above threshold (Sept 25 2026). No pricing adjustments attributed to AI. — [Neolytix medical billing cost guide](https://neolytix.com/articles/what-is-the-going-rate-for-medical-billing-services/)
- Tebra survey of 190 billing companies (Apr 23 2026): 59% haven't adopted AI; 63% now charge 7.99% or less of collections; 54% expect gross margins at or below 10%; 67% believe payers use AI to increase denials; denial rates rose to 46% from 28%; 68% of billing companies earn under $500K/yr (up from 42% in 2023); 98% offer add-on services. — [Tebra, Medical Billing Industry Outlook 2026](https://www.tebra.com/theintake/practice-operations/billing-companies/medical-billing-industry-outlook)
- RCM AI venture funding exceeded $500M in 2026 by August (snippet only). — [MedicalBillingOpportunity](https://www.medicalbillingopportunity.com/resources/weekly-news/ai-medical-billing-business-opportunity-2026/)

**Contingency debt collection**

**Analyst statement of the mechanism**
- Emergence Capital's AI-native services playbook (Mar 30 2026) lists four price supports: outcomes-based pricing ("the service IS the outcome"), trust and credibility, switching costs from deep integration, and data flywheels; it names good targets as insurance claims and brokerage, fund administration, customs brokerage, RCM, KYC compliance and "stitch work" BPO. — [Emergence Capital playbook](https://www.emcap.com/thoughts/the-ai-native-services-playbook)
- Caritas: in accounting/tax rollups the "regulatory review layer keeps professional liability manageable" and practitioners "remain valuable for judgment/client relationships." — [Caritas AI rollup market map, Sept 2026](https://caritas.ventures/ai-rollup-market-map/)

### Inferences
- The common thread in holding categories is that the customer is buying a transfer of risk (who signs, who is insured, who gets paid only on success) rather than a quantity of labor. AI lowers the provider's cost to deliver, but the price is anchored to the customer's recovered dollars or to the licensed signature, not to hours.
- Medical billing is the most fragile of the "holding" categories: the percentage model survives, but the rate is drifting toward ≤8% and 54% of billers expect ≤10% gross margins. The $500M+ of 2026 RCM venture money is aimed at health systems and physician groups, which leaves the thousands of sub-$500K billing companies serving small practices as the segment most exposed to a new AI-native entrant pricing at, say, 3–4% of collections.
- Contingency property-tax and R&D-credit work hold price because the customer's alternative is DIY, not a cheaper vendor; the $49 AppealDesk product shows the DIY floor has moved from "free but laborious" to "$49 and easy," which will cap contingency pricing on small residential parcels first.

## Key Question 3: Economic theory and analyst views on which services hold price when production cost goes to zero

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 evidence converges on four mechanisms: (1) automation vs. augmentation — price and employment collapse where AI performs the whole task, hold or rise where it complements a human (Brynjolfsson et al. 2025/2026; Indeed Hiring Lab Sept 2026); (2) Baumol-style bottlenecks — essential tasks that resist automation absorb a growing share of spend (Aghion-Jones-Jones); (3) liability and licensing — unsigned output is a commodity, signed output is not (SOC 2, tax, Crosby); (4) pricing-model shift — VCs now model AI services at 50–60% gross margin and push toward outcome-based pricing, which only works where results are measurable and low-variance (Bessemer Feb 2026, Foundation Capital Sept 2026, ICONIQ 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Stanford Digital Economy Lab Canaries update (Aug 12 2026): workers 22–25 in highly AI-exposed occupations show employment ~19% below expected (up from 15% at July 2025); "Declines are concentrated in occupations where AI usage tends to automate human tasks. In occupations where AI is used more to complement workers, employment is flat or rising"; "adjustment is showing up primarily in employment rather than base pay." — [Stanford DEL, Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)
- Original paper (Aug 2025, ADP payroll data): early-career customer-service workers down nearly 11% from Nov 2022 peak to July 2025; youngest software developers 20% below late-2022 peak; health aides (low exposure) rose in all age groups. — [ADP Research summary, Aug 26 2025](https://www.adpresearch.com/main-street-macro/yes-ai-is-affecting-employment-heres-the-data); [paper PDF](https://cehd.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Brynjolfsson-etal-Canaries-AI-HO-2025-11-06a_jbb.pdf)
- Indeed Hiring Lab (Sept 17 2026): "AI Exposure Isn't Squeezing Advertised Pay in the US — It's Boosting It" (Jack Kennedy); a Sept 24 2026 piece warns AI's near-term bite "is more likely to be felt in the paychecks of degree-holding workers." — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/)
- Hui, Reshef & Zhou: ~2% fewer contracts, ~5% lower earnings for AI-exposed freelancers; "high-quality workers being disproportionately affected" — quality premium does not protect in commoditized tasks. — [WashU Olin](https://olin.washu.edu/about/news-and-media/news/2023/08/study-ai-tools-cause-a-decline-in-freelance-work-and-incomeat-least-in-the-short-run.php)
- Aghion, Jones & Jones (NBER, "Artificial Intelligence and Economic Growth"): Baumol's cost disease implies growth is constrained not by what we do well but by what is essential and yet hard to improve — automatable tasks' price share falls toward zero while bottleneck tasks absorb spending. (PDF fetched but not machine-parseable; cite with that caveat.) — [NBER chapter](https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c14015/revisions/c14015.rev1.pdf)
- Applied Baumol/AI discussion in healthcare (Apr 15 2025, search only). — [Forbes Tech Council](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbestechcouncil/2025/04/15/can-ai-cure-healthcares-cost-disease/)
- Bessemer (Feb 2026 pricing playbook): "Companies see 50–60% gross margins vs. 80–90% for SaaS. If the math doesn't work at 10 customers, it won't at 1,000." a16z (Casado/Bornstein) put AI margins "often in the 50–60% range." ICONIQ 2026 survey: average AI product margin 52% (up from 41% in 2024); pure application-layer resellers 45%; outcome-based pricing adoption 18% (up from 2% in Q2 2025); Sierra at $200M ARR and $15.8B valuation (May 2026) on outcome pricing. — [Digital Applied AI unit economics 2026 (citing Bessemer, a16z, ICONIQ)](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-unit-economics-pricing-margins-services-2026-framework)

### Inferences
- The framework that best predicts the tables below is: price holds where the deliverable is a signature, an insured promise, or a share of a measurable recovery; price collapses where the deliverable is a document or a dataset nobody signs. Baumol explains why spend migrates toward the former; Brynjolfsson's automation/augmentation split explains why employment (not wages) is where the adjustment shows.
- Outcome-based pricing is the VC-endorsed route for AI-native services to capture value, but Foundation Capital's own caveat (needs low inter-customer variance) means it is viable in collections, billing and tax-credit work, and weak in bespoke advisory — which is exactly where contingency models already exist.
- Analyst margin expectations have reset downward (50–60% for AI products vs. 80–90% SaaS). A services business that moves from ~50% to 60–70% gross margin is therefore the realistic prize, not "software margins."

### Inferences
- "Where not to go": anything in Table 1 whose deliverable is an unsigned document, transcript, translation, draft or list — the price has already fallen 90–99% and funded vendors sell it at near-zero marginal cost.
- "Where to look": Table 2 rows where the fee is a percentage of a measurable recovery or a licensed signature, and the "funded AI-native at small-business end" column reads "none" or "partial" — small-practice medical billing, small-balance collections, property-tax and incentive contingency work, compliance attestation, and small-firm legal.

### Gaps
- Insurance claims/TPA and property-management fee levels, traditional recruiting fee benchmarks, and practitioner-forum sentiment were not retrievable within the search budget and should be verified before relying on the corresponding Table 2 rows.
