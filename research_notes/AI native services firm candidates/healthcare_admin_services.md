# Outsourced Healthcare Administrative Services Bought Recurring by Small US Practices (candidates for an AI-native services firm, 2026)

Scope note: US market, state as of October 2026. Earlier-dated data is flagged. Research method: ~60 web searches/fetches across vendor pricing pages, market-research summaries, CMS/OIG/AMA primary documents, YC company pages, and funding coverage. The session's web-search budget ran out before several niche gaps (home-health fee %, pharmacy reconciliation pricing, credentialing market size, Latent Health funding, SLP/OT-specific fees) could be closed; those are listed as gaps rather than guessed. Many pricing sources are billing-vendor blogs (marketing content); where ranges agree across several independent vendors I treat them as reasonably reliable, and I flag single-vendor claims.

---

## KQ-A. Service-by-service evidence on the eight criteria (pricing model, revenue per client, digital share, fragmentation, licensing, reachability/switching, AI/EHR compression, market size and AI-native activity)

### Takeaway
Physician/therapy billing (RCM) is the only large category where small practices routinely pay an outcome-tied fee (4-10% of collections) that reaches the $20K+/client/year target at group-practice scale; credentialing, prior auth, scribing, MIPS, HIPAA and coding audits are mostly priced per unit of labor or flat, and patient bad-debt collections is outcome-tied but needs state collection-agency licensing. Funded AI-native RCM entrants (Adonis, Akasa, SmarterDx, Anka, CombineHealth, LunaBill) overwhelmingly target health systems and large groups; the ones nearest small practices are Candid Health (venture-backed digital-health providers), Toothy AI (dental), and Manta (specialty procedural practices, per-job pricing).

### Cited Findings

#### A1. Medical billing / RCM — general pricing and structure
- Outsourced medical billing "typically costs 4–9% of net collections"; vendor table: Primary Care/Family Medicine 4–6%, Internal Medicine 4–6%, OB-GYN 5–7%, Orthopedics 5–7%, Dermatology 5–7%, Neurology 6–8%, Pain Management 7–9%, ASC 6–9%, Mental Health/Behavioral 5–8% — [MedicalBillersAndCoders, 2026](https://www.medicalbillersandcoders.com/blog/how-much-does-medical-billing-cost/)
- Search-result aggregation (exact page not verified) across [Carepatron](https://www.carepatron.com/blog/how-much-do-medical-billing-services-cost/), [HealixRCM](https://healixrcm.com/how-much-does-medical-billing-cost) and [PRCPMD](https://www.prcpmd.com/post/medical-billing-company-rates): most practices pay 5–7%; Family/Internal Medicine 3–5%, Behavioral Health/Psychiatry 4–7%, Physical Therapy 4–6%, Radiology 5–8%, Anesthesia 6–9%, Urgent Care 3–5%, DME/HME 5–9%.
- "Outsourced RCM: typically 5–8% of collections for independent practices"; smaller practices' "true cost" of billing approaches 15% until they reach 500–600 claims/month — [GetPracticeHelp (cites MGMA DataDive)](https://www.getpracticehelp.com/blog/medical-practice-overhead-costs/)
- AMA data cited by vendors: solo practices spend an average of 10.9% of collections on in-house billing, vs 5–8% outsourced — [EliteMed Financials](https://elitemedfinancials.com/mental-health-billing-services-cost/); [CEcomputech](https://www.cecomputech.com/guides/behavioral-health-billing-cost/)
- Fragmentation: IBISWorld counts 1,364 US medical billing services businesses (2024), declining 3.4% CAGR 2019–2024; "highly fragmented with no companies holding a market share greater than 5%"; industry revenue $4.3B (2026) — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/number-of-businesses/medical-billing-services-united-states/)
- Market size: US medical billing outsourcing market $6.95B (2025) → $17.69B (2033), 12.56% CAGR 2026–2033; physician offices segment CAGR 14.4% "owing to the fragmentation of the provider landscape and resource constraints"; hospitals 46.97% share; outsourced component 58.41% — [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-medical-billing-outsourcing-market)
- Licensing: "States don't require medical billers and coders to be licensed"; CPB (AAPC) and CMRS (AMBA) are optional credibility credentials; billing companies are HIPAA Business Associates; New Jersey is cited as requiring a third-party billing service license — [MedRevenueHub](https://medrevenuehub.com/how-to-start-a-medical-billing-business-in-the-usa/); [Research.com state licensing](https://research.com/advice/state-licensing-differences-for-medical-billing-coding-degree-graduates); [NJ note](https://www.healthcaresystemcareersedu.org/new-jersey-medical-billing-and-coding/)
- Liability: OIG's Compliance Program Guidance for Third-Party Medical Billing Companies (63 FR 70138, Dec 18, 1998) states "The OIG has a longstanding concern that percentage billing arrangements may increase the risk of upcoding and similar abusive billing practices" and that for billing companies that also provide marketing services "percentage arrangements may implicate the anti-kickback statute"; it lists False Claims Act exposure and names risk areas (billing for services not rendered, upcoding, duplicate billing, unbundling, "insurance only" billing) — [OIG PDF](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/805/thirdparty.pdf)
- Switching triggers: high costs without added value, non-transparency on denials/collections, repeated invoicing errors and slow response, unmet profitability goals, poor reputation — [360Connect](https://www.360connect.com/supplier-blog/top-5-reasons-physicians-switch-medical-billing-providers/); practices switch when cash slows, denials rise, fees rise, reporting is unclear — [MedCare MSO](https://medcaremso.com/blog/know-everything-before-switching-medical-billing-companies/); vendor claims that 2026 triggers include OBBBA Medicaid eligibility changes in 19 states and the Jan 2026 CMS Prior Authorization API rule — [MedicalBillersAndCoders switching playbook (vendor claim)](https://www.medicalbillersandcoders.com/blog/switch-medical-billing-companies/)
- Denials: 41% of providers see denial rates ≥10% (Experian Health 2025 State of Claims, up from ~30% in 2022); 65% of denied claims never resubmitted; 60–70% of denials stem from front-end eligibility/data errors — [OmniMD 2026 denial report](https://omnimd.com/blog/average-claim-denial-rates-by-specialty/)

#### A2. Billing by specialty
- Behavioral health: fees "typically 6% to 8%", overall 3–8% / 5–10% — [EliteMed](https://elitemedfinancials.com/mental-health-billing-services-cost/), [Medisys](https://www.medisysdata.com/blog/mental-health-billing-services-cost-what-providers-need-to-know/); third-party therapist billers charge "6–8% of collections", i.e., ~$10,500–14,000/yr on $150–200K — [Verimedix](https://www.verimedix.com/simplepractice-billing-outsource-therapists/). Denial rate 10–16% (OmniMD) and 12–20% ("nearly double the 5–10% average for medical/surgical") — [blueBriX](https://bluebrix.health/blogs/the-top-reasons-behavioral-health-claims-denial). Revenue: Heard 2026 report (n=1,950, 2025 data): solo therapist median revenue $75,000; group-practice owner median $169,121; 74.6% accept insurance; insurer reimbursement $95–125/session; median 18 clients/week — [Heard](https://www.joinheard.com/resources/the-heard-2026-financial-state-of-private-practice-report); insurance pays ~$111/session vs $159 private pay — [Blueprint](https://www.blueprint.ai/blog/private-practice-costs). EHR bundling: SimplePractice managed billing ≈6% of insurance collections with $750/month minimum, in-network claims only (excludes OON, EAP, aging AR, appeals, credentialing) — [Mentalyc](https://www.mentalyc.com/blog/simplepractice-vs-therapynotes), [Verimedix](https://www.verimedix.com/simplepractice-billing-outsource-therapists/); TherapyNotes offers no managed billing ($0.14/e-claim) — [Mentalyc](https://www.mentalyc.com/blog/simplepractice-vs-therapynotes). Platform absorption: Headway's effective take reported at 10–15% of reimbursement, Alma charges $125/month flat; Headway cut therapist pay up to 30% on Jan 1, 2025 — [TherapyDial](https://therapydial.com/providers/setup/alma-vs-headway-payout-rates/), [Mental Wealth Solutions](https://mentalwealthsolutions.org/blog/headway-alma-therapist-pay-cuts-marketplace-platforms).
- ABA therapy: "Most ABA billing companies charge between 4% and 8% of collected revenue"; outsourcing recommended at 15–20+ active clients; generalist billers show denial rates 8–12 points higher than ABA specialists; manual auth tracking loses 4.2 sessions/client/yr — [Pacemave (vendor)](https://www.pacemave.com/post/aba-billing-services-the-complete-guide-for-therapy-practices). A search-summary claim of "as low as 3% for AI-powered ABA billing" could not be traced to a verified page.
- Physical therapy (and OT/SLP via the same rehab billers): fee 4–6% (aggregated above); WebPT's own RCM service is 6.5% of monthly collections plus a one-time fee, and WebPT is "the most widely adopted EMR in outpatient physical therapy" processing 11M+ claims/visits per year — [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/emr-software/webpt), [WebPT](https://www.webpt.com/products/rcm-service), [PatientStudio](https://patientstudio.com/blog/jane-app-alternatives-for-physical-therapy-practices). Revenue: $280–380K net revenue per therapist FTE is a healthy benchmark; <$220K signals under-scheduling — [Proactive Chart](https://www.proactivechart.com/resources/realistic-pt-productivity-benchmarks-for-outpatient-practices-in-2026/); average receipts per clinic $871K, net margin 14–20% — [Tupelo SMB](https://www.tupelosmb.com/blog/buying-a-physical-therapy-clinic-industry-overview); public comparable $106.49 net rate per PT visit Q1 2026 — [Proactive Chart](https://www.proactivechart.com/resources/realistic-pt-productivity-benchmarks-for-outpatient-practices-in-2026/). Denials: PT 11% with 45 days in A/R; 8-minute rule and missing modifiers are main causes — [MedicalBillingOpportunity](https://www.medicalbillingopportunity.com/resources/weekly-news/medical-billing-specialty-demand-2026/); chiro/PT 15–20% "when Medicare modifier logic breaks down" — [MedPrecision](https://www.medprecisionbilling.com/resources/denial-rate-by-specialty-2026/). Jane App "starts to show real limitations" for US insurance-billing PT practices — [PatientStudio](https://patientstudio.com/blog/jane-app-alternatives-for-physical-therapy-practices).
- Chiropractic: 5–10% of collections or $1,000–3,000/month flat (hybrid also offered) — [Streamline Billing Group](https://streamlinebillinggroup.com/chiropractic-billing-service-charges/); 4–9% — [SpryPT](https://www.sprypt.com/blog/chiropractic-billing-services); average practice >$700K annual collections per 2025 Chiropractic Economics Salary & Expense Survey (as cited by SpryPT); flat fees from ~$200/month solo — [EMR Guides](https://emrguides.com/chiropractic-medical-billing-service/).
- Dermatology: 5–7% fee; "cosmetic-vs-medical mix, audit frequency" — [MedicalBillersAndCoders](https://www.medicalbillersandcoders.com/blog/how-much-does-medical-billing-cost/); denial 6–12% (OmniMD) vs "14% — nearly 3x the industry average" — [Muni Health](https://muni.health/blog/dermatology-claim-denials-2025); overhead 45–55% (MGMA via GetPracticeHelp); eClinicalWorks RCM case: 120-provider derm group at 99% net collection — [BusinessWire 2023](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20231012472612/en/120-Provider-Dermatology-Practice-Reports-a-99-Net-Collection-Rate-With-eClinicalWorks-Revenue-Cycle-Management-RCM).
- Primary care: 4–6% fee; MGMA median collections per primary-care physician $312,427 (all practices, 2023 data), $392,162 with 0% technical component — [MGMA Provider Compensation 2024 report PDF](https://mgmatraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/ProviderSpecialtyRollUps2024.pdf); denial 5–10% (OmniMD); overhead 60–70% (MGMA via GetPracticeHelp). Family practice has "lower per-claim denial rates (~8%)" and generic ICD-10 coding — [MedicalBillingOpportunity](https://www.medicalbillingopportunity.com/resources/weekly-news/medical-billing-specialty-demand-2026/).
- Pediatrics: denial 7–12% (OmniMD); no specialty-specific fee found (gap); Candid Health lists Modern Pediatrics and Little Otter as customers — [Candid Health](https://www.joincandidhealth.com/).
- Urgent care: fee 3–5% (aggregated); denial 8–12% (OmniMD).
- Anesthesia: 6–9%, "very high complexity" — [Neolytix](https://neolytix.com/articles/what-is-the-going-rate-for-medical-billing-services/), [HealixRCM](https://healixrcm.com/how-much-does-medical-billing-cost).
- Radiology: 5.5–10% professional / 2–5% global; 5–8% — same sources; denial 15–22%, driven by prior auth and medical-necessity scrutiny (OmniMD).
- Pathology/lab: contracts 4–8%, labs aim to cap at 6–7% — [Neolytix](https://neolytix.com/articles/what-is-the-going-rate-for-medical-billing-services/).
- EHR-bundled billing elsewhere: athenahealth charges 4–7% (some sources 4–8%) of collections for athenaCollector, with practices under 3 providers at 5–7% — [Business.com](https://www.business.com/reviews/athenacollector/), [BusinessNewsDaily](https://www.businessnewsdaily.com/16305-athenahealth-medical-billing.html); eClinicalWorks is $449/provider/month EHR or $599 with PM (flat) — [Emitrr](https://emitrr.com/blog/athenahealth-vs-eclinicalworks/).

#### A5. Prior authorization services
- Pricing is per-unit or per-FTE: $5–15 per PA request; per-auth from $4–8; flat plans from $500/month; dedicated PA specialist $399/role/week ($349 at volume); in-house PA coordinator $52–78K fully loaded; outsourcing becomes rational at 75+ PAs/week — [Staffingly](https://staffingly.com/medical/services/prior-authorization/), [Outsource Strategies](https://www.outsourcestrategies.com/insurance-prior-authorization/), [HealthCell](https://healthcell.com/articles/when-should-small-practice-consider-outsourcing-prior-authorizations/); four models (per-request, hourly, dedicated specialist, managed service) — [HireInSouth](https://www.hireinsouth.com/post/prior-authorization-outsourcing)
- Burden (AMA 2025 survey, n=1,000 physicians): practices complete 40 PAs per physician per week; physicians and staff spend 13 hours/week on PAs; 40% have staff working exclusively on PA; 95% report care delays; 32% say PAs are often/always denied; 74% say denials increased over five years; only 21% always appeal, 52% cite insufficient staff to appeal; 60% worry AI will raise denial rates — [AMA PA survey PDF](https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/prior-authorization-survey.pdf)
- Regulation: CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule (CMS-0057-F) has an initial Jan 1, 2026 implementation date and a Jan 1, 2027 deadline for FHIR Prior Authorization APIs — [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/burden-reduction/interoperability/policies-and-regulations/cms-interoperability-and-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f)
- Outcome-tied AI entrant: Manta Health (founded 2025; $1.3M seed June 2026, Amplify.LA) automates eligibility, PA, appeals and financial clearance for "specialty procedural practices" and "charges based on outcomes, not usage... a flat fee per job completed", with a human expert biller for AI failures — [Manta](https://manta.health/), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/1394612-83). Larger entrants: Tennr ($101M Series C June 2025, $605M valuation, ~$162M total; referral intake, benefits checks, PA) — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/06/18/tennr-health-tech-ai-patient-referral-ivp-a16z-lightspeed-iconiq-series-c/); Infinitus ($102.9M total; voice agents for benefits/PA calls; payers, pharma, providers); Notable ($119M; health systems); Assort Health ($222M+; specialty practices front office) — [NewMarketPitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/healthcare-ai-agents-startup)
- Market: AI-based PA market $1.50B (2025) → $4.21B (2031), 19.4% CAGR — [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6254168/ai-based-prior-authorization-market-share); US AI PA automation $0.63B (2025) — [SNS Insider](https://www.snsinsider.com/reports/ai-prior-authorization-automation-market-10559)

## KQ-1. Which specialties have the simplest coding and highest denial pain, making them easiest for a non-clinical team to learn and most willing to switch billers?

### Takeaway
Behavioral health, outpatient rehab (PT/OT/SLP), ABA and chiropractic combine a narrow CPT set with above-average denial rates (10–20%) driven by administrative causes (eligibility, authorizations, session limits, modifiers, the 8-minute rule), which is the profile most learnable by a non-clinical team; primary care and urgent care have low denial rates and low fees, while anesthesia, radiology, pathology, dermatology and home health carry clinical/coding complexity that commands higher fees but is harder to learn.

### Cited Findings
- Behavioral health denials 10–16% driven by "policy-driven constraints, session limitations, authorization requirements" — [OmniMD](https://omnimd.com/blog/average-claim-denial-rates-by-specialty/); 12–20%, "nearly double" med/surg — [blueBriX](https://bluebrix.health/blogs/the-top-reasons-behavioral-health-claims-denial)
- PT denials 11%, 45 days A/R; main culprits are the 8-minute rule and missing modifiers (administrative, not clinical) — [MedicalBillingOpportunity](https://www.medicalbillingopportunity.com/resources/weekly-news/medical-billing-specialty-demand-2026/); chiro/PT 15–20% from Medicare modifier logic — [MedPrecision](https://www.medprecisionbilling.com/resources/denial-rate-by-specialty-2026/)
- 60–70% of all denials stem from front-end eligibility and patient-data errors; 85%+ are preventable; 65% are never resubmitted — [OmniMD](https://omnimd.com/blog/average-claim-denial-rates-by-specialty/)
- Family practice has ~8% denials and "generic ICD-10 coding" while neurology leads with 14% initial denials from professional/technical component splits — [MedicalBillingOpportunity](https://www.medicalbillingopportunity.com/resources/weekly-news/medical-billing-specialty-demand-2026/)
- Primary care 5–10%, urgent care 8–12%, pediatrics 7–12%, dermatology 6–12% (medical-vs-cosmetic disputes), radiology 15–22% (prior auth/medical necessity), ASC 12–20% — [OmniMD](https://omnimd.com/blog/average-claim-denial-rates-by-specialty/)
- ABA: generalist billers show denial rates 8–12 points higher than ABA specialists; practices lose 4.2 sessions/client/yr to manual authorization tracking — [Pacemave (vendor)](https://www.pacemave.com/post/aba-billing-services-the-complete-guide-for-therapy-practices)
- Fee as complexity signal: anesthesia 6–9% "very high complexity", radiology professional 5.5–10%, pathology 4–8% — [Neolytix](https://neolytix.com/articles/what-is-the-going-rate-for-medical-billing-services/); pain management 7–9%, neurology 6–8% — [MedicalBillersAndCoders](https://www.medicalbillersandcoders.com/blog/how-much-does-medical-billing-cost/)
- Therapist willingness to switch: SimplePractice's bundled billing excludes denials/appeals, OON, EAP and aging AR, and therapists report third parties offer "more comprehensive denial management" — [Verimedix](https://www.verimedix.com/simplepractice-billing-outsource-therapists/); Headway cut pay up to 30% in 2025, prompting therapist misgivings about platforms — [Mental Wealth Solutions](https://mentalwealthsolutions.org/blog/headway-alma-therapist-pay-cuts-marketplace-platforms), [ClearHealthCosts](https://clearhealthcosts.com/blog/2025/11/therapists-have-misgivings-on-the-platforms-alma-headway-etc-and-the-business-of-therapy/)

### Inferences
- "Simple coding + high denial pain" is concentrated in therapy-type specialties (BH, PT/OT/SLP, ABA, chiro): each uses a handful of time-based or session codes, and the denial drivers are eligibility, auth tracking, visit limits and modifiers, which are process problems that software plus a non-clinical team can own.
- Primary care/urgent care are easy to learn but have the lowest fees (3–6%) and lowest denial pain, so the switching motive is weaker; derm/anesthesia/radiology/path have higher fees but need coding expertise (a CPC partner would be required to credibly sell).

### Gaps
- No survey quantifying annual billing-vendor switching rates by specialty was found; switching evidence is qualitative (vendor blogs, forums).

## KQ-5. What are the compliance and liability risks that a product team must plan for in each category?

### Takeaway
Every category runs under HIPAA Business Associate obligations; billing carries False Claims Act and anti-kickback exposure (OIG has a stated "longstanding concern" about percentage billing arrangements), patient collections requires state licenses, bonds and compliance with 30+ state medical-debt laws, prior auth and scribing create clinical-accuracy liability, and New Jersey licenses third-party billers.

### Cited Findings
- OIG 1998 guidance for third-party billing companies: percentage arrangements "may increase the risk of upcoding and similar abusive billing practices"; percentage arrangements plus marketing services "may implicate the anti-kickback statute"; risk areas include billing for services not rendered, upcoding, unbundling, duplicate claims, "insurance only" billing; qui tam exposure under the False Claims Act — [OIG PDF (63 FR 70138)](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/805/thirdparty.pdf)
- Billing companies are HIPAA Business Associates; no state professional license except New Jersey's third-party billing service license — [MedRevenueHub](https://medrevenuehub.com/how-to-start-a-medical-billing-business-in-the-usa/), [NJ note](https://www.healthcaresystemcareersedu.org/new-jersey-medical-billing-and-coding/)
- Collections: state licensing and surety bonds (MA $25K, CA $25–50K, TX $10K, KS), 30+ states with medical-debt statutes, mandatory charity-care screening/payment plans in several states — [Tratta](https://www.tratta.io/blog/debt-collection-licensing-guide-requirements), [RCR Hub](https://rcrhub.com/hubbub/medical-debt-collection-laws-compliance-guide), [Commonwealth Fund](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/fund-reports/2025/jul/state-protections-against-medical-debt-look-policies-across-us), [Nevada NRS 649](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-649.html)
- Prior auth: 26% of physicians report PA led to a serious adverse event; 60% fear AI-driven denials — [AMA 2025](https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/prior-authorization-survey.pdf)
- Coding audits: audit teams "often hold active credentials including CPC" — [Staffingly](https://staffingly.com/medical/services/medical-coding-services/); CPC is the AAPC standard credential — [CPC guide](https://medicalbillingandcodingclasses.org/certifications/cpc/)
- MIPS: incorrect reporting affects Medicare payment adjustments; many small practices are exempt under the ≤$90K/≤200-patient low-volume threshold — [Healthmonix](http://healthmonix.com/mips-eligibility/)
- HIPAA compliance software from $199/month covers SRA, policies, BAAs — [Accountable](https://www.accountablehq.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- A percentage-of-collections firm should document a compliance program following the OIG 1998 guidance (coding integrity, no marketing-plus-percentage combinations, refund of overpayments) and keep coding decisions with the provider or a CPC partner.
- Patient collections (bad-debt stage) is impractical for an unlicensed 2–4 person firm across states; early-out statements as part of billing (before accounts become delinquent) avoids most licensing, but this needs legal confirmation per state.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full list of states requiring third-party biller registration beyond New Jersey; could not confirm whether early-out/pre-delinquency patient statements on behalf of a provider require a collection license in each state.

#### Top 3 in this category (ranked by score with evidence; not a recommendation)

1. Behavioral-health group-practice billing (35/40). Justification: fees are outcome-tied at 5–8% of collections ([MedicalBillersAndCoders](https://www.medicalbillersandcoders.com/blog/how-much-does-medical-billing-cost/), [EliteMed](https://elitemedfinancials.com/mental-health-billing-services-cost/)); a 10-clinician group collecting ~$1M yields $50–80K/yr; the work is entirely digital (claims, portals, payer calls); denial pain is high (10–20%) and administrative in nature (eligibility, auth, session limits) ([OmniMD](https://omnimd.com/blog/average-claim-denial-rates-by-specialty/), [blueBriX](https://bluebrix.health/blogs/the-top-reasons-behavioral-health-claims-denial)); the CPT set is narrow; no license is required; 74.6% of therapists accept insurance ([Heard](https://www.joinheard.com/resources/the-heard-2026-financial-state-of-private-practice-report)); SimplePractice's bundled billing excludes denials/appeals/OON/EAP and carries a $750/month minimum, leaving a wedge for third parties ([Verimedix](https://www.verimedix.com/simplepractice-billing-outsource-therapists/)); supplier base is 1,364 small billing firms with none >5% share ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/number-of-businesses/medical-billing-services-united-states/)). Strongest counter-evidence: solo therapists (86.5% of the market, median $75K revenue) are far below the $20K/client threshold; SimplePractice already bundles billing at ~6% and Headway/Alma absorb the entire billing function with 10–15% effective takes ([TherapyDial](https://therapydial.com/providers/setup/alma-vs-headway-payout-rates/)); Candid Health ($120M Series D) lists behavioral-health digital providers (Mood Health, Ophelia, Little Otter, Mantra) as customers ([Candid](https://www.joincandidhealth.com/)); and OIG has a stated concern about percentage billing arrangements ([OIG](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/805/thirdparty.pdf)).

2. Chiropractic billing (35/40, tied; ranked second on smaller per-account size). Justification: 5–10% of collections or $1–3K/month flat ([Streamline](https://streamlinebillinggroup.com/chiropractic-billing-service-charges/)); the average practice collects >$700K ([SpryPT citing Chiropractic Economics 2025](https://www.sprypt.com/blog/chiropractic-billing-services)), so one average client is a $35–56K account; denial pain is concentrated in Medicare modifier logic and runs 15–20% ([MedPrecision](https://www.medprecisionbilling.com/resources/denial-rate-by-specialty-2026/)); no dominant EHR bundles RCM the way WebPT/SimplePractice do (Jane is weak for US insurance billing — [PatientStudio](https://patientstudio.com/blog/jane-app-alternatives-for-physical-therapy-practices)); chiropractors are MIPS-eligible ([Healthmonix](http://healthmonix.com/mips-eligibility/)) which creates an add-on. Strongest counter-evidence: the $700K figure is total collections including cash-pay, and no source quantifies the insurance-billed share, so the billable base may be much smaller; solo chiropractors are offered flat fees from ~$200/month ([EMR Guides](https://emrguides.com/chiropractic-medical-billing-service/)) which signals price compression; no market-size or AI-entrant data specific to chiropractic was found (C8 scored on inference).

3. Outpatient rehab (PT/OT/SLP) billing (34/40). Justification: 4–6% third-party fees ([aggregated](https://www.carepatron.com/blog/how-much-do-medical-billing-services-cost/)) on $280–380K per therapist FTE ([Proactive Chart](https://www.proactivechart.com/resources/realistic-pt-productivity-benchmarks-for-outpatient-practices-in-2026/)) gives the largest per-clinician fee pool among therapy specialties (a 5-PT clinic is a $70–120K account); denials (11%, 45 A/R days) come from the 8-minute rule and modifiers, which are rule-based ([MedicalBillingOpportunity](https://www.medicalbillingopportunity.com/resources/weekly-news/medical-billing-specialty-demand-2026/)); fully digital; no license. Strongest counter-evidence: WebPT is "the most widely adopted EMR in outpatient physical therapy", processes 11M+ claims/visits per year, and sells its own RCM at 6.5% of collections ([SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/emr-software/webpt), [WebPT](https://www.webpt.com/products/rcm-service)), so the incumbent platform already captures the bundle; PT clinics average only $871K receipts ([Tupelo SMB](https://www.tupelosmb.com/blog/buying-a-physical-therapy-clinic-industry-overview)), so most are 2–3 therapist shops; and the SLP/OT-specific fee and revenue data could not be found.

Close runner-ups: ABA billing (34) with 4–8% fees and explicit specialist-vs-generalist denial gap but CentralReach-centric ecosystem and no revenue benchmark found; dental billing+verification (33) with $1M+ practices and 4–9% fees but Henry Schein/eAssist scale and Toothy AI targeting; prior authorization (31) as the best non-billing option with 40 PAs/physician/week and Manta's per-job pricing model but regulatory (CMS-0057-F) and funded-competitor (Tennr, Infinitus) pressure.

### Inferences
- Scores on C6 (reachability) rely on qualitative switching triggers; the actual annual churn of billing vendors is unquantified, so C6 differences between specialties are judgment calls.
- C8 for chiropractic, ABA and urgent care is inferred from general physician-office growth (14.4% CAGR) because no segment-specific market sizing was found.

### Gaps
- Segment-level market sizes for BH/PT/chiro/ABA/dental billing outsourcing were not found (only total US billing outsourcing and dental vendor counts).
- No evidence on actual annual vendor-switch rates, average contract lengths, or termination terms in small-practice billing agreements.
- SLP/OT, pediatrics, urgent care, home health and hospice fee percentages are unverified; anesthesia/radiology/pathology small-group collections are unverified.
