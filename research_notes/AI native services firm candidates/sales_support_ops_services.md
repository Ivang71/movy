# Outsourced Sales, Marketing, Customer Support and General Operations Services for US SMBs: Candidates for an AI-Native Services Firm (state as of October 2026)

Scope note: this file covers the sales / marketing / customer support / general operations slice of the larger "AI-native services firm candidates" effort. Research method: web search plus direct page fetches of pricing pages, vendor terms, comparison guides and trade press, October 2026. Where a figure is dated it is marked; where a source is a vendor or a vendor-adjacent comparison blog (most pricing data in this space is), that is flagged. The session's web-search budget was exhausted before every category could be sourced; unsourced items are listed as Gaps rather than guessed.

Scoring scale used throughout (5 = best for a 2-4 person, software-skilled, capital-light, unlicensed, remote firm):
- C1 Pricing model: 5 = contingency/commission/per-outcome is the market norm; 3 = hybrid or per-transaction; 1 = hourly/seat/retainer-for-hours.
- C2 Annual revenue per client to provider: 5 = >$50k; 4 = $20-50k; 3 = $10-20k; 2 = $5-10k; 1 = <$5k.
- C3 Digital share: 5 = fully digital; 1 = mostly physical.
- C4 Fragmentation: 5 = thousands of small providers, no dominant platform at the small-customer end; 1 = dominated by platforms or well-funded AI-native startups.
- C5 Licensing: 5 = none required.
- C6 Buyer reachability / sales cycle: 5 = buyers self-identify online, weeks-long cycle; 1 = hard to identify, quarters-long.
- C7 AI price compression: 5 = no evidence AI software is compressing the service price; 1 = AI product already sells the outcome at roughly a tenth of the service price (treated as disqualifying).
- C8 US market size / growth / activity: 5 = large and growing with room; 1 = small or shrinking.

## Key Question 1: Which of these services keep an outcome-tied price that AI software has not collapsed, and why?

### Takeaway
The outcome-tied prices that have held are the ones where the provider is accountable for recovering money or booking a human-verified result against a counterparty that demands evidence: Amazon Vendor Central deduction/shortage recovery (15-30% contingency, 5-year lookback), parcel/freight invoice audit (25-50% of recovered credits), telecom/utility bill audit (percentage of savings), and pay-per-qualified-meeting SDR work ($150-$800 per meeting). Prices in those categories are set by the recovered dollars, not by labor hours, and the AI products that exist there either charge the same contingency (Chargeflow 25%, Stripe Smart Disputes 30%) or have failed to deliver the outcome reliably (AI SDR churn 50-80%).

### Cited Findings
- Amazon Vendor Central recovery: ProfitGuard charges "15% fee on the money we successfully recover" and calls that "significantly cheaper than the industry standard of 25-30%"; audits "up to 5 years" of history; cites data that "93% of brands are losing 5% of their revenue" to Amazon deductions; targets "£1M+ brands" — [Wake Commerce ProfitGuard](https://www.wakecommerce.co.uk/profitguard-amazon-chargeback-recovery)
- Vendor Central shortage claims "sometimes equalling 5-10% of all shipped volume"; third-party services "reclaim up to 70% of these fees"; claims recoverable "going back up to five years"; Amazon "hides shortage details older than 9 months from the interface"; disputes "can take up to 35 days or longer" — [Wake Commerce shortage claims guide](https://www.wakecommerce.co.uk/blog/amazon-vendor-central-shortage-claims); [asgtg Vendor Central fee reconciliation](https://asgtg.com/vendorcentral/) ("Only Pay For Results", five-year window, "increase profitability by up to 20%")
- RefundPros Vendor Central service: performance-based only, "no upfront fees", recovers shortage claims, "invalid or excessive chargebacks", co-op discrepancies, pricing/invoice deductions, freight/compliance deductions, duplicate deductions; targets "Amazon 1P vendors with frequent purchase orders" — [RefundPros](https://refundpros.com/vendor-central-reimbursement-services/)
- Carbon6 extended into 1P vendor recovery by acquiring chargeguard (May 2023) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230504005455/en/Carbon6-Extends-Profit-Recovery-Solutions-to-1P-Vendors-with-chargeguard-Acquisition)
- Parcel audit: "going market range is 25-50% of recovered credits"; services "recover 2-5% of shipping spend"; worked example: $500k UPS/FedEx spend, 3% recovery = $15k, at 50% contingency the auditor earns $7,500 — [Darrigo Consulting parcel audit guide](https://www.darrigoconsulting.com/blog/parcel-audit-services-guide); [ShipSigma small parcel audit](https://shipsigma.com/small-parcel-audit)
- Parcel audit benchmarks: hard billing errors are only "0.2-0.7% of parcel spend"; refund-eligible service failures "~3.5% of volume"; named contingency providers Shipware, AFS Logistics, ICC Logistics; software/subscription platforms Sifted, Reveel, Loop; "no public head-to-head benchmark exists" — [Rocket Fuel Recharge parcel audit comparison](https://rocketfuelrecharge.com/blog/parcel-audit-companies)
- Telecom/utility audit: "no-risk model, meaning businesses only pay a fee if savings are identified"; "recover 10-30% in overcharges"; audits reach back "36-48 months" — [Cost Analysts](http://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/); [National Utilities Refund](https://nationalutilitiesrefund.com/audits/telecom-bill-audits/); [Auditel](https://auditelinc.com/telecom-audit-services/)
- Outsourced SDR pay-per-meeting: "$150-$800 per meeting", enterprise "can exceed $1,000"; retainers $2,500-$4,000 (entry), $4,000-$7,500 (mid), $7,500-$15,000+ (enterprise); outsourced rep "$42,000-$60,000 per representative annually" vs in-house "$125,000-$150,000" — [Whistle outsourced SDR pricing guide 2026](https://www.whistle.ltd/post/outsourced-sdr-pricing-guide-2026); pay-per-meeting "$175 to $350 per qualified meeting" — [Outbound Sales Pro](https://outboundsalespro.com/outsourced-sdr-pricing-2025/); "$150-$600 for mainstream B2B ICPs" — [Rose Talent Solutions](https://rosetalentsolutions.io/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost)
- AI SDR tools have NOT replaced that price: "AI SDR churn is reported at 50 to 70% annually"; head-to-head test "human SDRs generated $147,000 against AI at $56,000", show rates 71% vs 52%; "No performance-based (pay-per-meeting) pricing models identified among these vendors" — [Forma Norden AI SDR pricing](https://formanorden.com/blog/ai-sdr-pricing/)
- TechCrunch (March 2025) reported former employees alleging 11x churn of 70-80% (11x claimed 79% retention), ARR "counting full-year contracts even after customers terminated at the 3-month break clause", and listing ZoomInfo and Airtable as customers when they were not; 11x raised $24M Series A (Benchmark, Sept 2024) and $50M Series B (a16z, Nov 2024) — [SalesMotion summary of TechCrunch report](https://salesmotion.io/blog/turns-out-ai-sdrs-are-too-good-to-be-true-11x-might-face-legal-action)
- Chargeback management: Chargeflow "25% Per Recovered Chargeback", "$29 per Deflected Chargeback", "4x ROI Guarantee", "20,000+" brands — [Chargeflow pricing](https://www.chargeflow.io/pricing); Stripe Smart Disputes "30% of the disputed amount for each dispute you win. There's no Smart Dispute fee for lost disputes." — [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing); Justt charges "a percentage of recovered funds", $100M raised — [Chargemate 2026 review](https://www.chargemate.tech/articles/chargeback-management-companies-2026)
- Amazon FBA reimbursement contingency: Getida "twenty-five percent (25%) of all reimbursements", first $400 free — [AMZFinder Getida review](https://www.amzfinder.com/blog/getida-review/); Seller Investigators / SPS Revenue Recovery 25%, "Refully charges around 18% and TrueOps near 10%" — [Hackceleration Carbon6 review](https://hackceleration.com/labs/review/carbon6)
- Failed-payment recovery: Butter Payments is "a revenue share on recovered invoices" (percentage undisclosed), FlyCode "pay-on-recovery", Vindicia and FlexPay revenue share — [FlyCode 2026 comparison](https://www.flycode.com/blog/top-payment-recovery-platforms-2026-comparison-chart-success-rate-stats); Gravy "custom pricing", "human-driven recovery service" — [Recurflux Churnkey alternatives](https://recurflux.com/resources/guides/churnkey-alternatives-2026)
- Amazon 3P account management: "percentage of revenue typically ranges from 8-15% of your monthly Amazon sales"; fixed $1,000-$12,000+/month; hybrid "$2,000 for core services plus ... 3% of PPC-attributed revenue" — [SalesDuo](https://salesduo.com/blog/amazon-account-management-services-cost/); [Azterion](https://azterion.com/en-us/amazon-account-management-cost/)
- Pay-per-lead: "$20 to $200 per lead"; legal "$100-$600+ per qualified lead"; home improvement "$45 to $228" — [Post Affiliate Pro](https://www.postaffiliatepro.com/faq/how-much-to-pay-per-lead/); [Landerlab CPL benchmarks](https://landerlab.io/blog/cost-per-lead-by-industry); [Aged Lead Store home improvement](https://agedleadstore.com/home-improvement-leads-cost-2025-guide-by-state-service-type/)

### Inferences
- The surviving outcome-tied prices share three properties: (a) a counterparty (Amazon Vendor Central, UPS/FedEx, a telecom carrier, a card issuer) that only pays on an evidence packet it adjudicates; (b) money already lost that the client cannot easily see (hidden shortage data, 0.2-0.7% invoice error rates spread across thousands of line items); (c) an accountable party that signs the claim. AI lowers the provider's cost to build the packet but has not changed what the buyer pays, because buyers pay a share of what comes back, not for hours.
- Where the platform that holds the money also sells the AI representment (Stripe Smart Disputes at 30%, Amazon's own proactive FBA reimbursements), the platform captures the contingency and the independent service is squeezed. Where the platform is the adversary (Amazon as the vendor's customer in Vendor Central; carriers in parcel billing) the independent service persists.
- Pay-per-meeting SDR is the one sales category where an outcome price survives, precisely because the AI-only products have demonstrably under-delivered the outcome (churn, show rates). This is a window, not a moat.

### Gaps
- Butter Payments' and Gravy's exact revenue-share percentages are not published anywhere fetched; only "revenue share" / "custom" could be verified.
- No source gave a documented share of SDR agencies that actually sell pay-per-meeting versus retainer.
- Procurement/vendor-management gainshare pricing for SMBs was not found (search budget exhausted); the closest sourced analogue is telecom/utility audit.

## Key Question 2: Which contingency-recovery services have fragmented providers and no dominant platform at the small-seller end?

### Takeaway
Of the five contingency-recovery categories examined, Amazon Vendor Central (1P) deduction recovery and parcel/freight invoice audit remain fragmented with no dominant AI-native platform at the sub-$5M-revenue end; FBA (3P) reimbursements are consolidating (Carbon6 sold to SPS Commerce for $210M) and shrinking under Amazon's 2024-2025 policy changes; chargebacks are already owned by funded AI-native platforms (Chargeflow, Justt) and by Stripe natively; failed-payment recovery is owned by the billing platforms themselves (Stripe Smart Retries is free).

### Cited Findings
- FBA reimbursement consolidation: Carbon6 "acquired by SPS Commerce for $210 million, with the deal closing in February 2025"; reimbursement product rebranded "SPS Revenue Recovery"; targets "established third-party FBA sellers, not beginners"; March 31, 2025 policy "structurally shrinks the amount any provider can recover" — [Hackceleration Carbon6 review](https://hackceleration.com/labs/review/carbon6)
- FBA policy timeline: October 23, 2024 lost/damaged claim window cut to 60 days; November 1, 2024 "proactive reimbursements for lost items" began automatically; March 2025 reimbursement basis changed to "product manufacturing costs"; Getida estimates sellers lose "1-3% of annual revenues" to unclaimed reimbursements ($10k-$30k on $1M) — [eComEngine FBA reimbursement landscape](https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/fba-inventory-reimbursement)
- FBA: "all removal claims will still need to be filed manually" even after automation; low-cost DIY software exists (ReimburseOps $0 free tier or $19/month, no commission) — [AMZFinder Getida review](https://www.amzfinder.com/blog/getida-review/)
- FBA provider fee spread (evidence of price competition, not fragmentation at scale): Getida 25%, Seller Investigators 25% (negotiable for large accounts), Refully ~18%, TrueOps ~10% — [RevenueGeeks Seller Investigators review](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/seller-investigators); [AMZBase Seller Investigators alternatives](https://amzbase.com/guides/seller-investigators-alternatives/)
- Chargebacks are platform-dominated: Chargeflow $35M Series A (Nov 2025, Viola Growth), $49M total incl. $10M debt, "over 30,000 connected stores", leads the Shopify ecosystem; Justt $100M total incl. $30M Series C late 2024; Chargebacks911 largest managed provider (settled with FTC and Florida in 2023; Capterra "$200 and up"); ChargePay flat $19.99-$99.99/month for Shopify; Chargeback.io $29 Ethoca / $15 RDR alerts, no minimum — [Chargemate](https://www.chargemate.tech/articles/chargeback-management-companies-2026); [Chargeback.io alternatives](https://www.chargeback.io/blog/best-chargeflow-alternatives)
- Chargeback market: "roughly $3.0 billion in 2026 ... $9.3 billion by 2035, with software and platforms making up 72.8% of that market, not services" — [Chargemate](https://www.chargemate.tech/articles/chargeback-management-companies-2026)
- Stripe Smart Disputes: "AI rules engine", "automatically submits the pre-filled evidence packet just before the dispute times out", "No integration required", fee only on wins — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/smart-disputes); fee 30% of disputed amount on wins; standard dispute fee $15 — [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)
- Failed-payment recovery is owned by billing platforms: Stripe Smart Retries "costs nothing extra on Stripe Billing" and "recovered over $6.5 billion in revenue for users in 2024" — [Slicker](https://www.slickerhq.com/resources/blog/best-revenue-recovery-tools-stripe-subscription-businesses); Paddle Retain bundled, Recurly and Chargebee retries bundled; Churnkey subscription from $300/mo for SaaS with "monthly churned revenue above $500"; Butter targets "mid-market to enterprise" — [FlyCode comparison](https://www.flycode.com/blog/top-payment-recovery-platforms-2026-comparison-chart-success-rate-stats); Churn Buster "$269/mo for one product, $430/mo for Dunning and Cancel Flows", "roughly 1% of your failed payment volume" — [Churn Buster](https://churnbuster.io/butter-payments-alternative)
- Vendor Central recovery providers found: ProfitGuard (Wake Commerce, UK, 15%), asgtg (results-only), RefundPros (results-only), Carbon6/SPS chargeguard, Flywheel Digital (agency), ClearChain (software) — [Wake Commerce](https://www.wakecommerce.co.uk/profitguard-amazon-chargeback-recovery); [asgtg](https://asgtg.com/vendorcentral/); [RefundPros](https://refundpros.com/vendor-central-reimbursement-services/); [Flywheel Digital](https://www.flywheeldigital.com/blog/amazon-chargebacks-fee-recovery-guide); [ClearChain](https://useclearchain.com/amazon-vendor-deductions); [BusinessWire chargeguard](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230504005455/en/Carbon6-Extends-Profit-Recovery-Solutions-to-1P-Vendors-with-chargeguard-Acquisition)
- Parcel audit providers: contingency firms Shipware, AFS Logistics, ICC Logistics; software Sifted, Reveel, Loop; managed-service Green Mountain Technology, TransImpact, Transportation Insight; hybrid Intelligent Audit; "no public head-to-head benchmark exists" — [Rocket Fuel Recharge](https://rocketfuelrecharge.com/blog/parcel-audit-companies); small-shipper caveat: "If you ship fewer than 100 packages per week, the dollar recovery from an audit may be modest" — [Darrigo Consulting](https://www.darrigoconsulting.com/blog/parcel-audit-services-guide)
- Telecom/utility audit: multiple small regional firms (Cost Analysts, Auditel, National Utilities Refund, Diversegy, OEO) all on contingency; none fetched claims a dominant national share — [Cost Analysts](http://www.costanalysts.com/services/utility-auditing/); [Auditel](https://auditelinc.com/); [Diversegy](https://diversegy.com/what-we-do/audits/); [OEO](https://oeo.com/utility-bill-audit/)

### Inferences
- Fragmentation ranking among contingency-recovery services (most to least open for a new entrant): Vendor Central deduction recovery > parcel/freight audit > telecom/utility audit > FBA reimbursements > chargebacks > failed payments. The first three have no funded AI-native entrant found in this research; the last three each have one or more $35M-$100M-funded platforms and/or free native platform features.
- The small-seller end of FBA reimbursements is now served by $0-$19/month software plus Amazon's own automation, so a service firm there would be competing with "free" for a pool Amazon has shrunk twice (60-day window, manufacturing-cost basis).
- Vendor Central's five-year lookback means the first engagement with any vendor is a large one-time backlog; recurring revenue thereafter depends on ongoing deduction volume (sourced at ~5% of revenue), which is still meaningful but smaller.

### Gaps
- No US market-size figure was found for Vendor Central deduction recovery or for parcel audit services specifically; only the chargeback and BPO markets had sized reports.
- The Carbon6/SPS $210M figure comes from a single review site; a primary press release was not fetched before the search budget ran out.
- Could not fetch Reddit r/FulfillmentByAmazon threads directly; seller sentiment on reimbursement services post-2025 is inferred from the review sites' "debate in 2026 about whether the 25% fee is still justified" rather than from first-hand seller posts.

## Key Question 3: Which categories are clearly dead for a new entrant because AI products now sell the outcome directly for a tenth of the price?

### Takeaway
Six categories fail the AI-compression test outright with documented ~10x price gaps: customer support outsourcing (Fin $0.99/resolution vs $15-$35 human ticket; Gorgias $0.90-$1.00 per automated interaction vs $12-$16/hour Philippine agent), transcription (AI $0.10-$0.30/min vs human $1.50-$4.00), translation (raw AI ~$0.002/word vs human $0.15-$0.30), data entry/catalog data entry ($0.20-$2.33 per SKU), review/reputation management ($9.95/location software vs $299-$449/location platforms), and local listings (Yext $199/year/location). FBA reimbursements and chargebacks are not "a tenth of the price" but are effectively dead for an independent entrant because the platform itself sells the automated outcome. SEO content is half-dead: article prices collapsed ($611 to $131 to $60-$75) while link and GEO prices held.

### Inferences
- The 10x test is met where the deliverable is a text/audio artifact (transcript, translation, listing copy, review reply, support answer) that the buyer accepts from software without a human signature. It is not met where the deliverable is money recovered from a third party or a human-verified meeting.
- AI SDR tools cut the sticker price (~$900-$3,750/month vs $4,000-$7,500 for a dedicated outsourced rep) but 50-80% churn and inferior output mean they have not compressed the price of the outcome; buyers still pay $150-$800 per qualified meeting when someone will stand behind the meeting.
- Customer support is the clearest "dead" category for a 2-4 person firm: it is simultaneously the most AI-compressed (88% cost reduction claims) and the most heavily funded (Decagon, Sierra, Fin, Gorgias) at every customer size from $10/month to $386k/year.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence was found of VA/executive-assistant prices falling because of AI; the VA guides fetched describe AI-trained assistants but not price declines. Scored 2 on C7 by inference (offshore rates of $4-$20/hour already undercut, and the tasks overlap with AI assistants), not by cited price data.
- Data-entry hourly benchmarks from Upwork/Fiverr Pro were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).

## Key Question 4: What do clients pay per year in each surviving category, with sources?

### Takeaway
Among categories that survive the AI-compression screen, annual provider revenue per client is: outsourced SDR $30k-$90k (retainer) or $42k-$60k per rep; Amazon 3P account management $12k-$144k (typically $24k-$42k mid-tier); Klaviyo/email-SMS retainers $36k-$144k; PPC management $18k-$120k; Vendor Central recovery tens of thousands on first-year backlog (15-25% of a 5%-of-revenue leak); parcel audit ~$7.5k per $500k of parcel spend at 50%; telecom/utility audit $2.5k-$7.5k per $50k of bills (inferred). Only SDR, 3P account management, email, PPC and Vendor Central reliably clear the $20k/client/year bar.

Top 3 (outcome-tied, AI-resistant, within the brief's constraints):

1. Amazon Vendor Central deduction/shortage recovery (34/40). Justification: the market norm is pure contingency at 15-30% of recovered funds with no upfront fee ([Wake Commerce](https://www.wakecommerce.co.uk/profitguard-amazon-chargeback-recovery); [asgtg](https://asgtg.com/vendorcentral/); [RefundPros](https://refundpros.com/vendor-central-reimbursement-services/)); the recoverable pool is large and persistent (shortages "5-10% of all shipped volume", "93% of brands are losing 5% of their revenue", five-year lookback) ([Wake Commerce shortage guide](https://www.wakecommerce.co.uk/blog/amazon-vendor-central-shortage-claims)); the work is entirely digital evidence assembly (BOL/POD attachment, invoice-level disputes, 35-day cycles) that a software-skilled team can automate internally while still charging on outcome; Amazon actively obscures the data ("hides shortage details older than 9 months"), which preserves the need for an accountable specialist; and the provider set is a handful of boutiques plus SPS/Carbon6's chargeguard rather than a funded AI-native platform. 3. Parcel / freight invoice audit & recovery (31/40). Justification: the market standard is contingency at 25-50% of recovered credits with no retainer ([Darrigo](https://www.darrigoconsulting.com/blog/parcel-audit-services-guide)); recovery requires line-item reconciliation of invoices against contracts and carrier surcharge schedules plus claim filing with the carrier, which is a pure data-and-evidence task suited to a software team; the provider landscape is fragmented across contingency firms, managed-service firms and subscription software with "no public head-to-head benchmark" ([Rocket Fuel Recharge](https://rocketfuelrecharge.com/blog/parcel-audit-companies)); no funded AI-native entrant selling the outcome at a fraction of the price was found. Strongest counter-evidence: hard billing errors are only 0.2-0.7% of parcel spend, so the $20k/client bar requires roughly $1M+ of annual parcel spend per client, which excludes most small businesses ([Rocket Fuel Recharge](https://rocketfuelrecharge.com/blog/parcel-audit-companies); "fewer than 100 packages per week ... modest" — [Darrigo](https://www.darrigoconsulting.com/blog/parcel-audit-services-guide)); subscription software (Sifted, Reveel, Loop) lets shippers self-serve, which is the same compression pattern that hit FBA reimbursements; and carriers control refund eligibility and can change terms (not sourced in this session, see Gaps).

### Inferences
- If the firm is willing to accept labor-priced-but-convertible categories, email/SMS management and Amazon 3P account management (33/40 each) are the best fallbacks: both have documented $20k-$144k/client/year, fragmented providers, and documented hybrid/revenue-share structures (3P management's 5-15% of sales; 3% of PPC-attributed revenue) that could be pushed toward outcome pricing.
- Telecom/utility audit ties on total with SDR and parcel audit but loses on revenue per client (C2 = 2) for SMB bills; it would only clear $20k with multi-site clients.

### Gaps
- Scores for procurement/vendor management (#22), inventory reconciliation (#20) and 3PL/freight admin (#21) rest on analogy, not sourced SMB pricing; they should be re-researched before any weight is put on them.
- Carrier-side changes to refund eligibility (e.g., money-back-guarantee suspensions) were not sourced; this is the main unquantified risk to the parcel-audit ranking.
- No source quantified how many US Amazon 1P vendors exist below $10M revenue, which is the key unknown for Vendor Central reachability.
