# Legal, Regulatory and Compliance Services: Outsourced Recurring Services for US SMBs, Law Firms and Professionals (candidates for a 2-4 person AI-native services firm, 2026)

Scope note: US market, state as of October 2026. Research was done with ~25 web searches and ~35 page fetches; the session's web-search budget was exhausted part-way through, so several gaps (practitioner-forum pricing, some funding rounds, some vendor pricing) could not be closed and are listed explicitly. Scores at the end are 1-5 on each of the eight assignment criteria, with the direction of each scale stated.

Legend used throughout: "per search summary" = the figure came from the search engine's synthesized snippet of the linked page and was not re-verified by fetching the page itself.

## Key Question 1: Which legal-adjacent services can be sold by non-lawyers in most states, with outcome-tied pricing and fragmented providers? (Service-by-service evidence on the eight criteria)

### Takeaway
Of the ~21 services enumerated, only a handful combine (a) no license or attorney gatekeeping, (b) pricing that attaches to a deliverable or deadline rather than hours, (c) fragmented small-provider supply, and (d) more than ~$20k/yr per client: data-privacy compliance programs (CCPA risk assessments / cybersecurity-audit readiness / DPO-as-a-service), ADA/WCAG remediation-plus-monitoring, GSA-Schedule / federal-contract compliance maintenance, and (borderline on revenue) medical-records retrieval for PI firms and OSHA retainers. The classic "paralegal-style" litigation-support services (demand letters, deposition summaries, chronologies, document review) are already price-compressed by funded AI products, and the filing-type services (registered agent, trademark, business licenses, process serving) are either platform-dominated, licensed, or too low-ticket.

#### F. Immigration paperwork preparation and UPL
- Federal definitions: "practice" is "exercising professional judgment to provide legal advice or legal services related to any matter before EOIR"; "preparation" is "the act or acts consisting solely of filling in blank spaces on printed forms with information provided by the applicant or petitioner" without professional judgment, and when done by non-practitioners "fees must be nominal and the individual cannot represent themselves as qualified in legal matters" — [8 CFR 1001.1 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/1001.1).
- The practice of immigration law "includes the selection and preparation of forms" and advice on how to answer questions; only attorneys and DOJ-accredited representatives may advise — [AILA / INS memo](https://www.aila.org/library/ins-role-of-visa-consultants-in-immigration) (per search summary).
- State rules: California — consultants "can translate forms for you to complete, but they cannot provide you with legal advice or tell you what form to use" — [State Bar of California](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/public/concerns-about-attorney/avoid-legal-services-fraud/unauthorized-practice-law); Maryland requires a posted sign "I am not an attorney licensed to practice law in Maryland, and may not provide legal forms, provide legal advice, or provide legal services" — [CLINIC Maryland](https://www.cliniclegal.org/node/1939) (per search summary); Michigan: a non-attorney "immigration clerical assistant may only charge $20 per page for translating supporting documents, and $10 a page for completing a government form" and "cannot ... pick which forms to fill out for you" — [State Bar of Michigan booklet (PDF)](https://www.michbar.org/file/professional/pdfs/uplimmigration.pdf).
- Attorney-backed consumer model: Boundless operates through "Boundless Legal LLC", "licensed in Arizona" and practicing "federal immigration law before USCIS", with attorney reviews, flat-rate pricing (page shows $780 and $1,335/$1,385 figures for marriage-green-card options), and claims "100,000+ people helped" and "99.7% success rate" — [Boundless](https://www.boundless.com/) (price labels were ambiguous on the page; treat as indicative).

#### G. Trademark and business filing services
- Who may practice: "Individuals who are not attorneys are not recognized to practice before the Office in trademark and other non-patent matters" (grandfather exception pre-1957); when a foreign practitioner represents a party, a US attorney "must also be appointed" — [37 CFR 11.14 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/11.14).
- The USPTO position (as quoted by a trademark firm): non-attorney filing services may not "consult or give advice to anyone in contemplation of filing a trademark application", "prepare or prosecute a trademark application", "sign amendments ... or responses to Office Actions", or appear before the TTAB; LegalZoom had claimed 250,000+ trademark filings "without the assistance of an attorney" (2018 article, updated Aug 2025) — [Gerben IP](https://www.gerbenlaw.com/blog/legalzoom-boasts-250000-trademark-filings-but-hires-a-prestigious-law-firm-to-file-its-trademarks/).
- LegalZoom now markets "LegalZoom Legal Services" as "the number one law firm filer by volume for US-based businesses" at the USPTO (i.e., it moved the service inside a law firm) — [LegalZoom](https://www.legalzoom.com/business/intellectual-property/trademark-registration-overview.html) (per search summary). LegalZoom revenue was US$681.9M in 2024; the North Carolina bar settled UPL litigation with LegalZoom in 2015 after a 2008 cease-and-desist — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LegalZoom).

## Key Question 3: Which compliance services are forced spend by regulation with deadlines, and who serves small businesses today?

### Takeaway
Dated, statutory obligations with penalties exist for CCPA risk assessments/cyber audits (2026-2030 phased), EPCRA Tier II (annual), OSHA recordkeeping/ITA submissions, FDA registrations and 510(k) submissions, customs entries, state license renewals, and Title II WCAG (2027-2028 for governments); private-sector WCAG is "forced" by litigation rather than a deadline. Small businesses are served by a long tail of boutique consultants plus a few platforms (Vanta/Drata/Delve, Encamp/ERA, Avalara/Harbor, accessiBe/AudioEye), and in OSHA's case by a free government program.

### Cited Findings

Evidence anchors for the scores are the findings in KQ1-KQ4; the principal ones per top-ranked row: CCPA deadlines and DPOaaS pricing ([CPPA](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html), [Engage Compliance](https://www.engagecompliance.co/outsourced-dpo-cost-guide)); WCAG lawsuit volume, monitoring fees and overlay floor ([ADAQuickScan](https://adaquickscan.com/blog/ada-website-accessibility-lawsuit-news-today-2025), [WCAGsafe](https://wcagsafe.com/blog/ada-compliance-cost-2026), [accessiBe](https://accessibe.com/pricing)); GSA maintenance retainers and FAR contingent-fee bar ([Winvale](https://info.winvale.com/blog/gsa-schedule-consulting-cost-faqs), [FAR 3.4](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-3.4)); records-retrieval flat fees and Datavant/Ontellus rollup ([RecordRS](https://www.recordrs.com/pricing/), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/108039-16)); SOC 2 spend and Delve/Vanta ([Comp AI](https://www.trycomp.ai/hub/soc-2-cost-breakdown), [Delve](https://www.delve.co/)).

Top three with justification and strongest counter-evidence:

1. Data privacy compliance services (CCPA risk assessments, cybersecurity-audit readiness, DPO-as-a-service). Justification: this is the only category found where a regulator has just created a dated, multi-year, revenue-tiered obligation (risk assessments from Jan 1, 2026 with submissions by April 1, 2028; cyber-audit certifications April 2028/2029/2030; ADMT Jan 1, 2027 — [CPPA](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html)), recurring spend is already priced at $2k-$5k/month for small companies ([Engage Compliance](https://www.engagecompliance.co/outsourced-dpo-cost-guide)), delivery is entirely remote, no license is required for the readiness/program work, and supply is a long tail of consultancies rather than a funded AI-native platform. Pricing can be tied to deliverables with deadlines (assessment filed, audit-ready by date). Strongest counter-evidence: the obligations bite mid-market firms first (the under-$50M tier's audit certification is not due until April 1, 2030), the program-build market is heavily served by privacy law firms and Big Four/FTI-type advisors ("auditor fees ... $30,000 to $80,000" — [Thoropass](https://www.thoropass.com/blog/ccpa-audit-cost-a-guide)), the cybersecurity audit itself must be independent (so the same firm cannot both build and certify), and the low end is being commoditized by $9-$59/month software ([Enzuzo](https://www.enzuzo.com/blog/best-ccpa-compliance-software)); advising on what the law requires edges toward legal advice and may need the licensed partner.

2. ADA/WCAG remediation and monitoring. Justification: demand is litigation-forced and growing (8,667 suits in 2025, +37% in H1 — [ADAQuickScan](https://adaquickscan.com/blog/ada-website-accessibility-lawsuit-news-today-2025), [Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/ada-web-accessibility-lawsuits-in-the-usa)), buyers are identifiable from public dockets and settlement agreements mandate 1-3 years of paid monitoring at $1k-$3k/month ([WCAGsafe](https://wcagsafe.com/blog/ada-compliance-cost-2026)); the work is code-level and fully remote, suits a software-skilled team, needs no license, and the Title II rule adds a government-buyer deadline wave in 2027-2028 ([ADA.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/)). Pricing can be fixed per remediation with a conformance deliverable (VPAT/audit) and a monitoring subscription. Strongest counter-evidence: overlay vendors have set a $490-$3,990/yr price anchor with "litigation support" bundled ([accessiBe](https://accessibe.com/pricing)) and AI code tools make remediation increasingly a client-side developer task; settlement data conflict ($5k-$75k vs $30k-$100k+), suggesting many defendants pay the plaintiff and do minimal remediation; the Title II deadlines appear to have slipped a year; and the private-sector market has no statutory standard, so "accountability" pricing cannot promise lawsuit immunity.

3. Government-contracting compliance maintenance (GSA Schedule post-award compliance, SAM/registrations, proposal support). Justification: recurring retainers of $1,250-$5,000/month ($15k-$30k/yr maintenance — [Winvale](https://info.winvale.com/blog/gsa-schedule-consulting-cost-faqs), [AIGovBid](https://www.aigovbid.com/blog/gsa-schedule-consultant-cost)) sit above the $20k target, the buyer universe is public (SAM/GSA eLibrary) and transactional, delivery is fully remote, no license applies, and supply is a long tail of boutique GovCon consultants with only early AI-tool entry. Strongest counter-evidence: outcome pricing is legally hemmed in by FAR Subpart 3.4, which voids contingent fees for securing contracts outside the bona-fide-employee/agency exceptions and inserts an anti-contingent-fee warranty in contracts ([FAR 3.4](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-3.4)); GSA charges no fees and SAM registration is self-service, so part of the market is paying for work the government offers free; demand is voluntary (no statutory deadline forces a firm onto a Schedule), and the AI proposal-writing tools publishing these cost guides are explicitly positioning to replace the consultant ([AIGovBid](https://www.aigovbid.com/blog/gsa-schedule-consultant-cost)).

Runner-up note: medical-records retrieval for PI firms (rank 4) scores well on license-free, per-deliverable pricing and reachable buyers, but the category is consolidating under PE/Datavant ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/108039-16)) and the adjacent summarization work is already compressed from $1.50-$4/page to $0.05-$0.25/page ([Chartely](https://www.chartely.com/blog/medical-chronology-services-cost)); its upside is as the physical-ish data-acquisition layer that the funded PI AI vendors (EvenUp, Eve, Supio) do not own.
