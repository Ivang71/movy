# Outsourced Finance and Accounting Back-Office Services for US SMBs (5–500 employees): Candidate Scan for an AI-Native Services Firm (as of October 2026)

Scope note: US market, state as of 2026 where sources allow; older data are dated inline. The firm is assumed to be 2–4 people with software/product skills, no domain background, minimal capital, no licenses, delivering remotely. This document scores and ranks; it does not recommend.

Method note: Evidence was gathered via web search (18 queries before the session's search budget was exhausted) and ~45 direct page fetches of pricing pages, trade-association pages, funding announcements, and market reports. Several high-value pages (IBISWorld industry pages, Gusto pricing, NSA fee survey, ACA International, Reddit) returned 403/404/405 and are listed under Gaps. Search-engine summaries that attributed a figure to a specific page are cited to that page; where the summary blended multiple sources the citation is to the listed page most likely to hold the figure.

## Key Question 1: Which finance services keep their price because the fee is tied to money recovered or liability accepted, rather than to hours?

### Takeaway
A distinct "recovery audit" family of services is priced as a share of money found or saved (utility bill audit 25–40% of recovery; merchant-processing fee audit gain-share; freight/parcel audit 29–50% of refunds; sales/use tax reverse audit 30–40% of refunds; AP recovery audit 10–40%; property tax appeal 25–40% of first-year savings; B2B collections 10–35% of amounts collected; R&D credit 15–30% of credit; WOTC 10–20% of credits; chargeback recovery 25% per recovered chargeback). Everything in the bookkeeping/compliance family (bookkeeping, AP processing, sales tax filing, payroll, tax prep, fractional CFO, lease accounting, 1099, grant writing) is priced to labor or per task, and grant writing explicitly prohibits contingency fees by professional ethics.

### Inferences
- The outcome-tied categories are all "find money the client did not know it was losing" audits or "collect money owed" services, which share a structural feature: the client has no budget line for them, so a share-of-recovery price is the only way to sell them. This is why their prices have held while labor-priced services are being benchmarked against software subscriptions.
- Among outcome-tied categories, only a few are recurring at the client level: B2B collections (new placements each month), utility/telecom/merchant-fee audits (ongoing savings share plus periodic re-audit), freight/parcel audit (continuous invoice stream), property tax appeals (annual in reassessment-heavy states). R&D credits and WOTC recur annually; reverse audits and AP recovery audits recur only every 3–4 years (statute lookback).
- Cost segregation and R&D credits sit in a legal gray zone on contingency: the IRS discourages contingent fees on original returns (Circular 230), and Ryan's 2014 win only covered refund claims/amended returns. Providers like Swanson Reed reject contingency outright; Pilot charges 20%. A new entrant should expect mixed norms.

### Gaps
- No source quantified Verisave's gain-share percentage or term; "10-30%" savings is the vendor's own marketing claim.
- No independent benchmark of what share of utility-audit fee revenue is one-time refund vs. ongoing savings share.
- No source on whether any states cap or ban contingent fees for sales/use tax refund consultants (Cherry Bekaert page retrieved via search summary only; full page returned 503 twice).

## Key Question 3: Which niche bookkeeping or audit-style services have fragmented local providers and no funded entrant at the small end?

### Takeaway
Fragmentation is the norm: IBISWorld counts 324,124 payroll & bookkeeping businesses and 5,618 debt collection agencies in the US (2025). Within audit-style services, utility bill audit, merchant-processing fee audit, telecom audit, and sales/use tax reverse audit show many small shops at the SMB end with consolidation only at the enterprise end (Cass, Arcadia/ENGIE Impact, Ryan/Onex, Calero/Tangoe). Law-firm trust (IOLTA) bookkeeping is similarly fragmented with no funded entrant found, but is priced to labor.

## Key Question 5: Licensing and credential requirements, and whether one credentialed partner suffices

### Takeaway
Recovery-audit services (utility, telecom, merchant fee, freight/parcel, AP recovery, reverse sales-tax audit, R&D study work) require no state license. Collections is the heavily licensed category: 37 states plus 4 municipalities license consumer collection; over 25 states license commercial collection agencies (2016 count), with bonds and NMLS applications; Illinois tightened third-party scope effective Jan 1, 2026. Property tax consulting is licensed in Texas (40 classroom hours, exam, sponsorship), though attorneys and CPAs are exempt there. Tax prep/EA/CPA credentials are needed to sign returns and represent clients; a single credentialed partner suffices for that.

### Cited Findings
- Consumer collections: "37 states and 4 municipalities require specific licenses and bonds"; bonds "$5,000 to $50,000+"; "you need a California license, regardless of your office location" to collect from a California debtor — [Harbor Compliance](https://www.harborcompliance.com/debt-collection-agency-license); [Surety1](https://surety1.com/bond_info/collection-agency-surety-bond/)
- Commercial collections: "There are currently over twenty-five states which require a commercial collection agency to be licensed" (2016); CCAA members must file surety bonds of "Minimum of $300,000" for trust balances up to $5M; state fees "from a few hundred dollars to over $1,000"; processing "one week to five months"; many states use the NMLS; cities of Buffalo, Yonkers, Chicago also license; state table lists e.g. Arizona ($1,500 initial, bond required, no exemption), Florida ($500, bond), North Carolina ($1,050, bond), Washington ($425–$825, bond), Wisconsin ($1,000, bond, exemption possible), Ohio and Kansas (no license; Certificate of Authority only) — [Credit Research Foundation / CCAA, 2016 PDF](https://www.crfonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Commercial-Collections-Licensing-CCAofA-4Q2016.pdf)
- "In many states, collection agencies can collect business-to-business debt without a license because most collection agency statutes are written around consumer debt... the exemption is a state-by-state conclusion, not a national rule" — [Cornerstone Licensing](https://cornerstonelicensing.com/answers/can-you-collect-commercial-debt-without-a-license); Colorado: agencies collecting only commercial debts "do not need a collection agency license" — [Colorado AG](https://coag.gov/licensing/collection-agency-regulation/)
- Illinois (effective Jan 1, 2026): third-party collectors "collecting on behalf of...others" require a license; first-party collectors excluded — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/illinois-revamps-the-scope-of-its-collection-agency-licensing-law)
- California expanded its Fair Debt Collection Practices Act to certain commercial debts — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/california-expands-scope-states-fair-debt-collection-practices-act-certain)
- Texas property tax consultants: must pass a TDLR exam and complete "at least 40 classroom hours"; non-real-estate-licensed applicants "must be sponsored by a senior property tax consultant or Texas Attorney"; "an active Texas Bar attorney or a Texas registered CPA may practice as a Property Tax Consultant without registering"; 24 hours CE per renewal — [TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/ptcfaq.htm); [TDLR pre-license](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/ptcprelicedulist.htm)
- Cost segregation: studies should be prepared by "qualified individuals (engineer or CPA with training)"; ASCSP/CCSP are voluntary credentials — [CostSegregationPricing.com](https://costsegregationpricing.com/); [RE Cost Seg](https://www.recostseg.com/post/cost-seg-pricing-5k-vs-15k)
- Utility audit: AAUBA's CUBA certification exists; no mandatory license stated — [AAUBA](https://aauba.org/utility-bill-auditing/)
- Grant writing: GPA code of ethics bars contingency pricing but no license — [Grantable](https://grantable.co/how-much-does-a-grant-writer-cost)
- IOLTA bookkeeping: bar rules govern the lawyer, not the bookkeeper; violations trigger "mandatory CPA audit fees in the $1,500 to $5,000 range" — [Firm Balance](https://firmbalance.com/2026/05/07/how-much-does-law-firm-bookkeeping-cost-in-2026/)
- R&D credit contingency and Circular 230: Swanson Reed declines contingency to stay "compliant with professional tax preparer standards" — [Swanson Reed](https://www.swansonreed.com/services/our-fees/); IRS flags contingency cost-seg fees as "potential Circular 230 violations" — [RE Cost Seg](https://www.recostseg.com/post/cost-seg-pricing-5k-vs-15k)

### Inferences
- For a 2–4 person unlicensed team, collections is the only outcome-tied category where licensing is entity-level (bonds, trust accounts, per-state applications) rather than person-level, so "one credentialed partner" does not solve it; a commercial-only book reduces but does not eliminate the burden (25+ states as of 2016; Illinois expanded scope in 2026).
- Reverse sales-tax audit and R&D studies need a CPA/EA only at the filing/representation step; one credentialed partner suffices, but contingency pricing on original returns carries Circular 230 risk.
- Utility, telecom, merchant-fee, freight/parcel, and AP-recovery audits require no license and no credentialed partner; the only "credential" is client authorization (letter of agency) to access bills/accounts.

### Gaps
- The CCAA commercial-collection licensing table is dated 2016; a current (2026) state-by-state list for commercial-only agencies was not retrieved.
- Property tax consultant licensing outside Texas was not surveyed.
- Whether any state restricts contingent fees for sales/use tax refund consultants remains unverified.

Top 3 by criteria (not a recommendation):

1. Merchant processing fee audit and interchange optimization (33/40). Fee is a share of verified savings for a defined term ([Verisave](https://verisave.com/how-it-works)), so price is tied to outcome rather than hours; the work is entirely statement and configuration analysis done remotely; no license or credential is required; buyers (any card-accepting business) are reachable with a free statement review; the only providers found are small firms (Verisave, Cost Analysts) and no funded AI-native entrant or roll-up was identified; and revenue scales with card volume, so a mid-sized retailer or restaurant group can exceed $20k/yr even if a 10-employee shop cannot. Strongest counter-evidence: every savings figure ("10-30%," "25%-35%") comes from vendor marketing with no independent benchmark ([Verisave](https://verisave.com/)); no market-size or firm-count data exist, which may itself signal a small category; a growing share of SMBs are on flat-rate processors (Square, Stripe, Shopify Payments) where interchange optimization has nothing to work on (inference, not sourced); and savings depend on processor cooperation that the auditor cannot control.

2. Utility bill audit, optionally bundled with telecom and waste (31/40). Fee is 25–40% of refunds plus first-year savings ([AAUBA](https://aauba.org/utility-bill-auditing/)); refunds reach back 12–36 months; the SMB end is served by many small firms with an optional certification rather than a license; the enterprise end has consolidated (Cass "51 Million invoices," Arcadia/ENGIE Impact "300+ automated audits on every bill" — [Cass](https://www.cassinfo.com/); [Arcadia](https://www.arcadia.com/acquiring-engie-impact)) but no funded entrant targets the SMB end; tariff and bill data are digital. Strongest counter-evidence: per-client revenue is weak for 5–500 employee firms (AAUBA: "a few hundred dollars on small accounts"), so the $20k/yr bar is reached only with multi-site clients or by bundling telecom/merchant/freight as Verisave does ([Verisave CPA Academy deck](https://cpaacademy.s3.amazonaws.com/PPT/verisave_5BusinessCosts.pdf)); the refund component is one-time; the enterprise tooling that already automates 300+ audit checks per bill could move down-market; and no US market size or growth data were found.

3. Sales/use tax reverse audit (overpayment recovery) (30/40). Contingent fees of 30–40% of refunds (up to 75%) with no fee if nothing is found ([Cherry Bekaert](https://www.cherrybekaertsalestax.com/news/reverse-audits-what-to-know-before-setting-the-contingent-fee)); refunds on 3–4 years of purchases can be large for manufacturers and multi-state businesses, so per-engagement revenue can clear $20k; the work is invoice and exemption analysis done remotely; no license is required (one CPA/EA partner suffices for filing refund claims), and Ryan's 2014 court win established that contingent fees on refund claims are permitted ([Wikipedia: Ryan LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_LLC)); no AI-native entrant was found, and the funded sales-tax startups (Numeral, Anrok, Kintsugi) sell forward-looking filing, not retrospective recovery. Strongest counter-evidence: it is episodic (statute lookback 3–4 years), so recurring revenue per client is low unless combined with ongoing compliance; the category requires deep state-specific taxability knowledge that a team with "no domain background" lacks; the consolidator at the top (Ryan, PE-backed, $715M revenue in 2021) and large CPA firms own the mid-market relationships; the buyer (controller/CFO of a manufacturer) is harder to reach and slower to close than an owner-operator; and the full Cherry Bekaert page could not be retrieved (503), so the fee range rests on search summaries of that page and [Sales Tax People](https://sales.tax/expert-articles/sales-tax-overpayment-how-to-find-it-and-get-your-money-back/).

Runner-up note: B2B commercial collections (29/40) matches the pricing thesis best of all (pure contingency, monthly placements, 5,618 fragmented agencies, AI entrants consumer-focused) but is held back by entity-level licensing and bonding in 25+ states ([CRF/CCAA](https://www.crfonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Commercial-Collections-Licensing-CCAofA-4Q2016.pdf)), with Illinois expanding third-party scope in 2026 ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/illinois-revamps-the-scope-of-its-collection-agency-licensing-law)).

### Inferences
- The brief's two hard filters (outcome-tied pricing; no software at a tenth of the price) eliminate 13 of 28 categories outright; what survives are small, unglamorous recovery audits whose main weakness is revenue per client, not price durability.
- Fractional CFO and IOLTA bookkeeping are the only labor-priced categories that score within 4 points of the leaders; their price durability depends on liability/judgment rather than recovery, which the brief treats as weaker.

### Gaps
- Scores for construction bookkeeping and merchant-fee audit market size are inferred for lack of data.
- No practitioner-forum evidence (Reddit r/Bookkeeping, r/smallbusiness) could be retrieved on why clients switch providers; NerdWallet's Reddit-derived pricing tiers are the only forum-sourced datapoint.
- Buyer reachability and sales-cycle length (C6) were not quantified by any source for any category; scores rest on who the buyer is and whether providers offer free initial audits.
